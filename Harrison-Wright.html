It is with profound sadness that we announce the passing of our beloved Mr. Harrison “Dutty Harry” Wright.

Though he has departed this earthly life, the memories he created, the love he shared, and the lives he touched will forever remain.

His Life

A life remembered with love

Today, we gather to remember and honor Mr. Harrison Wright, better known to many of us as “Dutty Harry.” It is difficult to find the words to capture a life as fully and as generously as he lived. He touched so many of us in ways we probably never fully realize.

Mr. Harrison was born to Louanna Gillett on September 8, 1968. He attended Grace Primary School and later went on to Sec Two.

In his early years, he worked as a bartender at Caye Chapel. That was only the beginning of a life shaped by hard work, steady character, and devotion to the people around him.

He later met the love of his life, Abby Williams, and they were married on November 6, 1996. Together, they built a life grounded in dedication and, most of all, family.

After leaving Caye Chapel, Harrison and Abby went on to live in San Pedro, where they began their family. Carshena was the first child, and from there his home filled with love, laughter, and joy.

Mr. Harrison found great joy in his seven children and was blessed with ten grandchildren, whom he cherished deeply. Family was an important part of who he was, and the memories he created with them will continue to live on.

A Man Who Could Fix Anything

His work was more than a job — it was part of who he was.

Mr. Harrison spent much of his life as a mechanic, a man who took pride in fixing things and making them work again.

He worked on golf carts, boats, motorcycles, and almost anything else that could be repaired. He had a natural ability to understand machines and find a way to get things working again.

But Harrison did more than fix machines. He had a way of fixing situations and helping people. Whether someone needed help with a problem, a machine, or simply needed someone to be there, he was willing to lend a hand.

His Story, His Name

A part of his family's story

Mr. Harrison was also known for a complicated story of names and identity, something many of us did not fully understand at first.

Some of you may remember how his last-name situation was spoken about over the years. On September 2, Cashema and her sister realized that only the younger brother and Cashema were registered with his last name, Wright.

In 2011, Harrison completed a deed poll and changed his last name from Gillett to Wright.

Yet, some of his other children remained registered as McField, a name that came from a man their grandmother married but later divorced.

Whatever the names on paper may have said, Harrison remained the same man to all of his children — steady, loving, and present.

His Legacy

What he leaves behind

Today, we honor Mr. Harrison not only for what he did, but for how he did it — with patience, perseverance, and love.

His kindness and wisdom were not limited to special occasions. They were lived out in everyday moments: in the way he worked, the way he cared, and the way he kept showing up for the people he loved.
